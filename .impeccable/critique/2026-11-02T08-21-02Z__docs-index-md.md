## Overall Impression

The site is visually calmer and more readable than the average VitePress blog, and the article typography is close to best-in-class for Chinese long-form reading. The homepage, however, spends its first two screens saying the same thing twice. The single biggest opportunity is to turn the second half of the homepage into evidence of a living practice — recent notes, current work, a point of view — and let the distinctive product card arrive earlier.

## What's Working

- **The reading system.** `.content-container` renders at 640px with 1.8 line-height, 38px H1, 24px H2, 19px H3, and a balanced 640 + 48 + 224 composition. Long Chinese prose reads comfortably in both themes.
- **Controlled topic color semantics.** Swift, Flutter, AI, and 易经 each hold one accent across icon, hover line, and link; gold is reserved for the product. The 1152px hero, feature grid, and showcase edges align.
- **A real product moment.** The 易起卦 card has its own material language — warm gradient, icon plinth, gold rule — and is the most memorable thing on the page.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The homepage routes to the same four topics twice and buries the product
**What:** `docs/index.md` exposes three hero actions and four feature cards; `.home-showcase__topics` then repeats the same four destinations, followed by five shortcut pills. The tab order contains three separate links to `/swift/` before the product actions.
**Why:** It creates indecision, redundant keyboard stops, and an unnecessarily long path. On mobile the product CTA lands at roughly 2,587px of a 2,889px page.
**Fix:** Choose one primary topic index. Replace the second four-item list with genuinely new information — latest notes, current projects, or a short "what I'm working on" section — and move the product card above that secondary content.

### [P1] Tertiary text fails WCAG AA contrast
**What:** `.topic__kicker` is 3.10:1 in light and 3.20:1 in dark at 10.88px; `.post-item__date` measures 3.10:1 light / 3.20:1 dark; the light-theme `.product__eyebrow` gold measures 2.51–2.61:1. Everything else in the main reading path clears 4.5:1.
**Why:** Publication dates, category labels, and product context become difficult to read for low-vision users; these are normal-size text failures, not decorative exceptions.
**Fix:** Use `--vp-c-text-2` for dates and kickers, or retune `--vp-c-text-3` to at least 4.5:1 in both themes. Darken the light-theme gold label until it clears 4.5:1.

### [P1] Several mobile interactive targets are below the 44px touch guideline
**What:** Expanded `笔记` sublinks are 32px high, `.home-showcase__shortcuts a` are 36px, the open-menu theme switch is 40×22px (below the 24×24 WCAG 2.5.8 minimum), and the blog title link is only 23px high while the surrounding 202px row is inert. Footer links are 24px high — at the minimum, not a violation.
**Why:** These are hard to hit one-handed and disproportionately affect users with motor impairments. The blog row is especially misleading because most of its visual surface looks actionable but is not.
**Fix:** Make the entire `.post-item` the link, give menu sublinks and shortcut pills `min-height: 44px`, and enlarge the theme control's hit area to at least 44×44px.

### [P2] Articles lack context at the top and a useful ending
**What:** `.vp-doc h1` is followed directly by the source quotation and body. Neither the published date nor tags from frontmatter appear near the title; `最后更新于` appears only at the bottom. The mobile article is 21,040px long, then ends with a date and legal footer.
**Why:** Readers arriving from search cannot judge recency before investing time, and a long read terminates without a route back into the site's body of work.
**Fix:** Add published date and tags beneath the H1, keep the update date at the end, and finish articles with a compact `更多文章 / 相关阅读` block.

### [P2] Prompt templates require extreme horizontal scrolling on mobile
**What:** In `/article/gpt-5-prompting-guide/`, the first prompt `<pre>` has a 1,022px scroll width inside a 325px viewport. The copy button helps people who only want the prompt, but readers must pan across roughly three screens to read it.
**Why:** These blocks contain prose instructions, not syntax-sensitive code. Horizontal panning breaks sentence comprehension and makes the article's most useful material the hardest to consume.
**Fix:** Introduce a `prompt` code-block variant with `white-space: pre-wrap` and safe wrapping, while leaving genuine source code horizontally scrollable. If scrolling remains, add a visible scroll affordance.

## Cognitive Load

**3 of 8 checklist items fail: moderate cognitive load.**

- **Fail — Single focus:** The hero presents three actions, then four feature cards. The primary task is split between learning, browsing work, and investigating a product.
- **Fail — One thing at a time:** A reader chooses among the feature cards, then meets the same four choices again under `从这里开始`.
- **Fail — Minimal choices:** Seven visible journeys in the first part of the homepage, five shortcut pills, seven primary desktop nav links, and twelve sublinks in the expanded mobile `笔记` menu.

- **Pass — Working memory:** Every destination is visible; no prior-screen memory is required.

## Emotional Journey

- **Opening:** Calm, credible, technically literate. The large wordmark, restrained glow, and topic-colored icons create a strong first impression.
- **Peak:** The gold 易起卦 card is the most distinctive moment and feels like a real product from a real maker.
- **Valley:** Repetition begins immediately after the feature cards. On mobile the same four topics occupy another ~855px, making the page explain itself twice.
- **End:** On a 2,889px mobile homepage the product CTA sits at roughly 2,587px, just before legal footer text. Articles end with an update date and footer, with no onward editorial path.
- **Peak-end judgment:** The intended peak is the product card, but many mobile visitors will remember repetition and a long descent instead.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent):** Dates and kickers fail contrast; several touch targets are undersized; the active nav page has no `aria-current`; the product section jumps to an `h3` without its own `h2`. The visible focus rings, skip link, landmarks, and heading anchors are good foundations.

## Minor Observations

- `探索笔记` promises notes generally but links directly to `/swift/`; name the destination or provide a notes overview.
- `GPT-5 提示指南` in the article H1 conflicts with `GPT-5 提示词指南` in the blog list and frontmatter.
- The product image is an app icon, but `alt="易起卦应用截图"` describes a screenshot.
- The 易起卦 heading is an `h3` with no local `h2`, weakening the screen-reader outline.
- Frontmatter declares tags, but neither article pages nor the blog list expose them.
- The `START HERE` and `PRODUCT` English eyebrows feel imported from a generic portfolio template; `从这里开始` and `产品` would match the site's voice.
- At 320px with classic desktop scrollbars, `body { min-width: 320px }` produces a 15px horizontal blank band; content itself does not clip.
- Active navigation is indicated by color only, with no `aria-current`.

## Questions to Consider

- If the four feature cards are already the way in, what information does the second four-item index add that justifies ~855px on mobile?
- Is `探索笔记` intended to be a universal notes entrance? If not, why not name the destination; if yes, where is the overview page?
- Should the distinctive 易起卦 card become the intentional payoff of a shorter homepage rather than a reward after repeated navigation?
- What would article endings look like if they treated the next relevant note as part of the reading experience rather than leaving it to browser history?
